How a picture in a High Roller message is set in the space that the large layout, or a 4 x 6 in card, gives it. Fit is the default. Fill: the picture is scaled until it covers all of the space and keeps its proportions, so what does not fit is cropped at the edges. Fit: the picture is scaled until all of it fits in the space and keeps its proportions, so the space can have empty margins. Stretch: the picture is scaled to exactly the size of the space, so nothing is cropped and there are no margins, but the picture can look squashed or stretched.

Twitch

Cheers of at least this many bits may style their message with HTML (colors, sizes, rotated text, tables). Scripts, links and anything loaded from outside are always removed. Smaller cheers print as plain text only, with no pictures. 0 = plain text for everyone. Each printer has a switch, Allow Twitch High Roller prints, that keeps it out of this.

Caps how long a Twitch High Roller message can print: up to (bits ÷ this number) inches, so at 10, a 100-bit cheer gets 10 inches. Shorter messages print in full. Longer ones fade out at the limit. 0 = no limit.
Prints a receipt when a Hype Train starts, when a new Conductor (the top cheerer) takes over, at every level up and when the train ends. It needs the four Hype Train triggers in Streamer.bot. They come with the 2.5.0 import. If you updated without importing again, add them under Twitch, Hype Train. Off stops all four, even with the triggers in place. The Hype Train test prints always print.
Twitch ends a Hype Train with an End event. If that event never reaches Printer Bot, it prints the end receipt itself this many seconds after the time the train was due to expire. A whole number from 90 to 300. 90 is the default. Hype Train events reach Printer Bot only from Streamer.bot 1.0.4 or newer, because Twitch stopped sending the old ones in January 2026.

YouTube

Super Chats of at least this amount, in your YouTube currency, may style their message with HTML by the same rules as Twitch High Roller cheers, pictures included. Smaller Super Chats print as plain text only, with no pictures. A Super Chat in another currency is converted with the exchange rates of the U.S. Treasury. 0 = plain text for every Super Chat. Each printer has a switch, Allow YouTube High Roller prints, that is off until you turn it on.

The currency of the YouTube High Roller threshold and of the amount per inch.
Caps how long a YouTube High Roller message can print: up to (amount ÷ this number) inches, so at 2.50, a 10.00 Super Chat gets 4 inches. Shorter messages print in full. Longer ones fade out at the limit. 0 = no limit.

All platforms

The longest a High Roller message can print, whatever bits per inch or amount per inch says. Applies to High Roller cheers and Super Chats. Gift sub lists are never shortened. 0 = the built-in limit of 16.7 inches. Up to 40. On Letter paper (8.5 x 11 in) a High Roller message also stops at the end of its page: one page is the most it can take there.

Good to know

Printer Bot loads the picture from the site when the message prints, so that site sees this PC's address. It does not look at what a picture shows: anyone whose cheer reaches the Twitch High Roller threshold, or whose Super Chat reaches the YouTube High Roller threshold, can make this printer print a picture from a site you switched on. Up to three pictures print per message. A picture that does not load in time prints as [image]. Pictures also show when Hide links is on; other links still read [link].

Replaces text that looks like a web address in a normal message with [link] on the receipt (not every bare domain name is caught). High Roller messages are not touched.
Stops a flood of free prints. Counts only Twitch channel point redemptions, Twitch raids and Kick raids. Cheers, subs, gifts, tips and Super Chats are never limited. 0 = no limit.
